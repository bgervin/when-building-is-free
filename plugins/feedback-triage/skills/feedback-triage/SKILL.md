# Feedback triage

When building is nearly free, cost no longer says no for you. Every request is affordable, so the roadmap goes to whoever pushes hardest unless a written strategy decides first. This skill applies that strategy to incoming feedback, and hands the best opportunities to the `feature-charter` skill as bets.

It **recommends; people decide.** Never close, label, comment on or charter anything until the owner approves the triage report. Research on automated feedback processing agrees: machines sort well, group unevenly, and should give decision makers insight rather than make the decision.

## The Needle Ladder, briefly

This skill shares a ladder with `feature-charter`. Rungs 1 to 4 are activity (shipped, discovered, adopted, retained); rungs 5 to 7 are results (task success, user outcome, business outcome). An opportunity names the result rung where users fail. Votes, mentions and usage are diagnostics, like activity rungs. The full guide is `references/needle-guide.md` in the `feature-charter` skill.

## The pieces

| Piece | What it is | File |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy file | Diagnosis, target and non-target segments, guiding policies, areas, horizon mix, rulings | `templates/strategy.md` (a `product-strategy` block) |
| Triage report | The six steps' output, for the owner to approve | `templates/triage-report.md` |
| Opportunity brief | One shortlisted opportunity: who struggles, at which rung, how many, which policy and horizon | `templates/opportunity-brief.md` (an `opportunity-brief` block) |
| Bet board | One page for leaders: betting, probing, tuning, decided, not betting, off plan | Generated by `scripts/triage.py board` |

## Workflow A: Write or update the strategy file

1. Look for an existing `product-strategy` block (usually `strategy.md` at the repository root or in `docs/`).
2. If there is none, draft one from what the team already has: strategy decks, planning docs, OKRs, README, past decisions. **Never invent strategy.** Mark every inferred line as a proposal and ask the owner to confirm.
3. Apply the policy test in `references/writing-strategy.md`: each policy should decide at least one real open issue (`decides`); the validator warns when one doesn't. Push back on goals posing as policies ("grow ARR 30%") and on a strategy that excludes no one.
4. Run `scripts/triage.py validate` and fix what the information allows.

## Workflow B: Triage a batch

Follow `references/triage-protocol.md`:

1. **Sort into areas** using the strategy's areas and labels.
2. **Merge duplicates**, counting distinct accounts, not mentions or votes.
3. **Check past rulings.** Matches without new evidence get a reply that links the ruling.
4. **Check strategy fit and flag failure modes** (`references/failure-modes.md`). Fit means a policy decides it, yes or no. If no policy decides it either way, that is the finding: ask the owner for the missing policy, or propose a ruling with `policy: none` and a `policy_gap` note.
5. **Shortlist per area.** Restate requests as opportunities and name the rung where the job fails. Rank by the strength of evidence that a result fails (telemetry first), then by accounts in the target segment.
6. **Place on a horizon**: tune (H1), bet (H2) or probe (H3). Check the mix with `scripts/triage.py mix`.

Write the report from `templates/triage-report.md`, with a brief for each shortlisted opportunity. Lead with the call the owner has to make. Report briefly: what you grouped, what you flagged and why, and the questions only the owner can answer.

## Workflow C: One new request

For a single new issue or an escalation ("our biggest customer needs X", "the CEO wants Y", "a competitor just shipped Z"):

1. Check it against rulings and existing groups. If it is a duplicate or already ruled out, draft the reply.
2. Check strategy fit and the failure modes. Name the flag and ask the flag's question from `references/failure-modes.md`.
3. If it survives, write a brief or add it to an existing one. Don't jump it ahead of the queue because of who asked.

## Workflow D: Brief to bet

When the owner approves a brief as a bet or a probe, hand it to the `feature-charter` skill:

- The opportunity and its users become `hypothesis.for` and `because`.
- The brief's `rung` becomes the needle's rung.
- The breadth, telemetry and quotes become `evidence`. These are the bet's drivers, re-checked at every review.
- The brief's policy, area and horizon, plus the brief's id, become the charter's `strategy` block.

Set the brief's `status` to `chartered` and its `charter` to the new id.

## Workflow E: The bet board and the leadership review

Generate the board with `scripts/triage.py board` and follow `references/bet-board.md`. The board's purpose is to make a kill on its review date read as the plan working:

- Decisions come first, before new bets.
- The Not betting column answers re-litigated requests.
- The Off plan section surfaces overdue reviews, sunsets never removed, a stale strategy and a horizon mix that has drifted.

## Rules

- Breadth in the target segment counts. Votes and mentions are diagnostics.
- Requests are solutions; rank the opportunity behind them.
- Every no is recorded as a ruling, with a policy, a reason and a reopen condition.
- A flag is information for the owner, not a veto.
- Requests from a non-target segment are a no or a `services` ruling, not roadmap.
- Re-check the drivers. A commitment whose reasons are gone is a zombie, whoever made it.

## Validator

`scripts/triage.py` is in this skill's folder. It needs Python 3.9+ and PyYAML (`pip install pyyaml`).

```bash
python scripts/triage.py validate strategy.md triage/ docs/specs/   # strategy, briefs, and charter links
python scripts/triage.py mix strategy.md docs/specs/                # active bets by horizon against the plan
python scripts/triage.py board strategy.md triage/ docs/specs/ --out bet-board.md
```

The validator checks:

- The strategy's kernel: diagnosis, segments, policies with a `decides` example, areas, a horizon mix that adds to 100, and complete rulings.
- That briefs cite real areas, segments, policies and rulings.
- That charters' `strategy` links resolve.

It warns when a policy reads like a goal, when a strategy excludes no one, and when a brief has fewer than three accounts in the target segment. Settings live in `.feedback-triage.yml` (strategy review limit 120 days, minimum accounts 3, mix tolerance 15 points, board window 90 days). Folder scans skip this skill's own templates.
